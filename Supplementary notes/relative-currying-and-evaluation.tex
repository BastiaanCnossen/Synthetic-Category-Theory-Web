\section*{Relative currying and evaluation}

The mapping-anima definition of a
\href{exponentiable-functors.html}{dependent product} gives an equivalence.
To use it in calculations with actual functors over a base, one must identify
its value on the names of those functors. That is the subject of this note.
We use the Chapter 1 structures and the comparison convention of
\href{supplementary-triangles-over-a-base.html}{Triangles over a base}.

Fix $p\colon S\to T$, $f\colon C\to S$ and a chosen dependent product
$\Pi C\to T$, with evaluation
$\varepsilon_C\colon p^*(\Pi C)\to C$ over $S$.
For $t\colon E\to T$, denote the defining equivalence by
\[
 U_t\colon\Map_T(E,\Pi C)\iso\Map_S(p^*E,C).
\]
For a functor $u\colon E\to\Pi C$ over $T$, write
$\operatorname{ev}(u)=\varepsilon_Cp^*u$, with its pasted triangle over $S$.
Only the displayed dependent products are assumed to exist.

\begin{lemma}
\label[lemma]{lem:Supplementary_Relative_Named_Evaluation}
There is an identification
\[
 U_t(\ulcorner u\urcorner)=\ulcorner\operatorname{ev}(u)\urcorner
\]
in the relative mapping anima.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
The actual functor $U_t$ is the composite of base change on relative
mapping animae with postcomposition by $\varepsilon_C$.
Base change on a named triangle names the pullback lift together with its
second-projection triangle. Postcomposition on a named triangle names
the pasted composite. Combine these two computations and the associator.
The resulting equality is in the relative mapping anima, so the base
triangle is retained throughout.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
\label[lemma]{lem:Supplementary_Relative_Transposition}
Every functor $w\colon p^*E\to C$ over $S$ has a transpose
$\lambda w\colon E\to\Pi C$ over $T$ with a comparison
$\operatorname{ev}(\lambda w)\simeq_S w$.
A comparison $\operatorname{ev}(u)\simeq_S\operatorname{ev}(v)$ induces
a comparison $u\simeq_T v$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Lift the name of $w$ through $U_t$, and decode the resulting point to a
triangle. The naming roundtrip and
\Cref{lem:Supplementary_Relative_Named_Evaluation} identify the name of its
evaluation with the name of $w$. Convert that identification to a comparison
of triangles.

For reflection, name the given comparison, use
\Cref{lem:Supplementary_Relative_Named_Evaluation} at both endpoints, and
reflect through $U_t$. Decoding gives a comparison over $T$.
This asserts the existence of that comparison; it does not prescribe its
image as higher comparison data under a separately chosen evaluation action.
The named evaluation computation used in both arguments is proved in
\Cref{lem:Supplementary_Relative_Named_Evaluation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
\label[lemma]{lem:Supplementary_Dependent_Action_Laws}
Given chosen dependent products for categories $C,D$ over $S$ and a
functor $a\colon C\to D$ over $S$, put
$\Pi a=\lambda(a\varepsilon_C)$. There is a comparison
\[
 \varepsilon_Dp^*(\Pi a)\simeq_S a\varepsilon_C.
\]
The induced functors satisfy
$\Pi(\operatorname{id})\simeq_T\operatorname{id}$ and, whenever the three
required dependent products are supplied,
$(\Pi b)(\Pi a)\simeq_T\Pi(ba)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
The evaluation comparison is the transposition computation of
\Cref{lem:Supplementary_Relative_Transposition}. For the identity law,
compare both evaluations with $\varepsilon_C$, using the base-change
identity comparison and the unit laws over the base, and reflect.

For composition, evaluation of a composite is compared with the evaluation
of its second factor after base change of its first. This uses the
\href{supplementary-base-change-of-triangles.html}{base-change composition
comparison}, including its base triangle. Apply the two evaluation
comparisons, reassociate over $S$, and obtain $(ba)\varepsilon_C$.
Reflection now gives the composition comparison over $T$.
These are the stated identity and composition comparisons, without an
assertion of additional higher coherence.
\end{proof}
