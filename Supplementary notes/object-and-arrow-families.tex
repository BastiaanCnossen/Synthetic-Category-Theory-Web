\section*{From object families to arrow families}

The \href{full-subcategories.html}{full subcategory spanned by a collection
of objects} contains exactly the arrows whose endpoints belong to that
collection. The formalization uses this observation for coherent families
of functors. We explain the endpoint calculation that makes this possible.
Use the Chapter 1 structures, the walking morphism, its endpoint data and
the Segal axiom. All categories are absolute.

Let $j\colon J\hookrightarrow C^\simeq$ be a collection of objects, with
$J$ an anima. Let $M_J$ be the pullback of
$\Map([1],C)\to C^\simeq\times C^\simeq$ along $j\times j$.
Write $e_0,e_1\colon\Map([1],C)\to C^\simeq$ for the endpoint maps and
$m\colon M_J\to\Map([1],C)$ for its inclusion.

\begin{lemma}
\label[lemma]{lem:Supplementary_Object_Endpoint_Lift}
Given $a\colon T\to\Map([1],C)$, maps $x,y\colon T\to J$ and
specified comparisons $e_0a=jx$ and $e_1a=jy$, there is a lift
$\widetilde a\colon T\to M_J$ with $m\widetilde a=a$.
The collection $M_J$ is closed under identities and composition.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Pair the endpoint comparisons. Together with $a$ and $(x,y)$ this gives
the full cone defining $M_J$. Pullback factorization supplies the lift and
its first-projection computation.
For an identity, use the same chosen object for both endpoints.
For a composable pair, the source of its composite is compared with the
first source and its target with the second target by the endpoint laws
for composition. These supply the required cone for the composite family.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
\label[lemma]{lem:Supplementary_Object_Family_Factorization}
Let $i\colon A\to C$ be a subcategory presentation of $M_J$.
Suppose $\Gamma$ is an anima and we have functors
\[
 h\colon\Gamma\to\Map(D,C),\qquad
 k\colon\Gamma\to\Map(D^\simeq,J),
\]
together with an identification $j_*k=r_Dh$, where $r_D$ is the action
on objects. Then there is a family
$\widetilde h\colon\Gamma\to\Map(D,A)$ with $i_*\widetilde h=h$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Let $a(h)$ be the action of $h$ on arrows, with values in
$\Map(\Map([1],D),\Map([1],C))$.
For either endpoint $e$, naturality of the mapping action under restriction
gives
\[
 (e^*)_*a(h)=j_*(e^*)^*k.
\]
The $e^*$ on the left is endpoint evaluation in $C$; the one on the right
is endpoint evaluation in $D$. This equation retains the given
comparison on objects.

Uncurry $a(h)$ to obtain a family of arrows indexed by
$\Gamma\times\Map([1],D)$. The two endpoint equations allow
\Cref{lem:Supplementary_Object_Endpoint_Lift} to lift this family through
$M_J$. Curry back, using that $\Gamma$ is an anima. Now $h$ and its
lifted arrow action form a cone for the subcategory presentation tested
at $D$. Factor that cone; its first computation is $i_*\widetilde h=h$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label[corollary]{cor:Supplementary_Full_Factorization}
Let $i\colon A\to C$ be a full subcategory and $f\colon D\to C$ a
functor. A specified factorization of $f^\simeq$ through $i^\simeq$
gives a factorization $\widetilde f\colon D\to A$ with
$i\widetilde f=f$. Any two such factorizing functors are identified.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Name $f$ and its coherent object factorization. They give a cone for the
mapping-anima pullback characterizing the full subcategory. Factor this
cone and decode its first-projection comparison. For two factorizations,
combine their comparisons with $f$ and reflect through the embedding $i$.
The uniqueness declaration linked here compares the underlying functors;
a prescribed comparison between the complete factorization triangles is
not separately asserted by it.
\end{proof}
